The Best Comedy Clubs in Manchester

From Northern Quarter staples to smaller new-act nights — find the room that suits you.

Manchester has always punched above its weight when it comes to making people laugh. This is the city that gave the circuit Peter Kay, Caroline Aherne, John Bishop and Sarah Millican long before anyone outside the North West knew their names, and it hasn't slowed down since. Whether you want a big room with a proper headline act, a scrappy free open-mic upstairs in a pub, or a seven-nights-a-week Northern Quarter institution, there's a night for you. Here's our pick of the best comedy clubs in Manchester right now.

The Frog & Bucket

Manchester's longest-running comedy club has been on the go since 1993, and it's still the first name most locals reach for when someone asks where to see stand-up in the city. Now settled at the top of Oldham Street on the edge of the Northern Quarter, the Frog has built its reputation on backing northern talent. Comperes and support acts are almost always local, even when a big touring name is topping the bill.

The club runs most nights of the week, each with its own personality. Mondays belong to Beat the Frog, the club's famous new-act competition where amateur comedians get five minutes to win the crowd over or get "croaked off," a night that's launched the careers of Jason Manford and John Bishop, among others. Thursdays are a calmer, more considered bill mixing pros with rising acts, while Fridays and Saturdays step things up with Barrel of Laughs, a full-strength weekend lineup of national and international names. There's food, a full bar, and a proper cabaret-style room that's earned its reputation as one of the best places to catch a future star before they hit the big time.

📍 102 Oldham Street, Manchester M4 1LJ

Creatures Comedy Club

The newest name on this list, and arguably the most ambitious. Tucked into the Northern Quarter on Oldham Street, Creatures Comedy Club (sometimes still called Creatures of the Night) is Manchester's only seven-nights-a-week comedy venue, run by Canadian comic Evan Desmarais, who also hosts and curates much of the bill. Expect upwards of fifteen shows a week across the venue, ranging from mixed bills and new-act showcases to an improvised comedy night and a post-show bar with karaoke to keep the evening going.

It's fast become a go-to spot for both international headliners passing through and North West talent cutting their teeth, with regulars including the likes of Mick Ferry and Rachel Fairburn. Creatures also throws its own comedy festival each summer, pulling in acts previewing their Edinburgh Fringe shows before they head over the Pennines, a genuinely good reason to check the listings even if you've never been before.

📍 42-44 Oldham Street, Manchester M4 1LE

XS Malarkey

Don't let the daft name fool you, XS Malarkey is one of the most respected independent comedy clubs in the country, and Joe Lycett has gone as far as calling it "the best comedy club in the world." Founded in 1997 by comedian and Coronation Street actor Toby Hadoke, who still hosts nearly every week, it's a not-for-profit club that has deliberately kept ticket prices low (£3 for members, £6 otherwise) throughout its history.

The club has moved venues more times than most people can keep track of, from a Fallowfield pub through Platt Chapel and the Bread Shed to its current home at 53two near Deansgate, but the format has stayed the same: a Tuesday-night mix of big names trying out new material alongside the best up-and-coming acts from across the North West. Early-career sets from Peter Kay, Alan Carr, Justin Moorhouse and Sarah Millican are all part of XS Malarkey folklore.

📍 53two, off Deansgate, Manchester

The Laughienda

Free comedy doesn't come much better than this. Held weekly at The Lion's Den in the Great Northern on Deansgate, The Laughienda is entirely donation-funded and has quietly built one of the most loyal followings on the Manchester circuit, plus a North West Comedy Award for Best Open Mic along the way.

Expect a line-up of anywhere from six to ten comedians in a single night, running the gamut from complete first-timers finding their feet to bona fide TV names quietly trying out new jokes, all rounded off with a proper headline set. The atmosphere is famously supportive rather than heckle-heavy, which makes it a great shout if you fancy dipping a toe into live comedy for the first time. It's popular, though, so turning up on spec is a gamble, book ahead if you can.

📍 The Lion's Den, Deansgate Mews, Manchester M3 4EN

Club 9 Comedy Club

The newest addition to Manchester's free comedy scene, Club 9 Comedy Club has built a loyal following with its sell-out nights at the Banyan Tree, and it's easy to see why. It's completely free to get in, professionally run, and regularly packs the room out, which says a lot for a night that's still finding its feet on the circuit.

The format keeps things simple and effective: a rotating bill of circuit comedians in a relaxed bar setting, with no ticket required and a bucket collection for the acts at the end of the night if you're enjoying yourself. Off the back of its success at the Banyan Tree, Club 9 has already started popping up at other Northern Quarter venues too, so keep an eye on their listings if your local show sells out.

📍 The Banyan Tree, Manchester

Top comedy clubs in Manchester: head to head

With so many strong options in the city, it's worth knowing how the big names actually compare. Here's how some of Manchester's best-known clubs stack up against each other.

The Frog & Bucket vs Creatures Comedy Club

Both sit within a few doors of each other on Oldham Street, but they offer genuinely different nights out. The Frog & Bucket is the more established club, with a traditional comedy club feel, proper theatre-style seating and a track record stretching back to 1993. This is where you'll catch long-serving circuit comedians and the bigger touring names, all delivered in a polished, professional setting.

Creatures Comedy Club, on the other hand, has a more genuinely Manchester feel about it. Local acts are guaranteed on the bill, and there are loads of new-act nights where you'll see some of the biggest names on the scene right now dropping in, particularly on a Wednesday. It's a wilder, more unpredictable atmosphere than the Frog, and all the better for it.

If you're after polished and professional, the Frog is your joint. If you want an authentic, wild night out with a real sense of discovery, Creatures is where it's at.

Whichever night you land on, Manchester's comedy scene has one thing in common across the board: a fierce loyalty to homegrown talent and a knack for spotting the next big name well before anyone else does. Book early where you can, and expect to leave with a new favourite comedian you'd never heard of an hour before the show started.

More comedy nights around Greater Manchester

Beyond the city centre clubs, there's a whole network of regular comedy nights running across Greater Manchester and the wider North West, many of them touring the same circuit of headline acts. Comedy In A Van, a local promoter, runs a rolling calendar of shows at pubs, sports clubs and social venues, a good option if you're happy to travel a little further out or fancy something with more of a community-hall feel than a purpose-built comedy club. Regular nights include:

  • Didsbury Comedy Club: Didsbury Comedy Club, south Manchester
  • Heatons Comedy Club: Heatons Sports Club, Stockport
  • Oldham Comedy Club: The Loom, Oldham
  • Off The Rails Comedy Club: Saddleworth
  • Glossop Comedy Club: The Crystal Ballroom, Glossop
  • Congleton Comedy Club: The Lion & Swan, Congleton
  • First Thursday Comedy: The Met, Bury
  • Lymm Comedy Club: Lymm Rugby Club
  • Comedy @ Players Theatre: Cheadle
  • Comedy @ Alsager Civic: Alsager
  • Padiham Comedy Club: Comedy @ The Lawrence, Padiham
  • Lancaster Comedy Club and Hornby Comedy Club: The Borough, Lancaster and Hornby Comedy Club, Lancaster (a little further afield, but firm fixtures on the same circuit)

These nights typically run monthly with a rotating cast of headliners and compères drawn from the same pool of North West circuit acts you'll see at the city-centre clubs, so they're worth keeping an eye on if you've worked your way through the Manchester regulars, or simply live closer to Stockport, Oldham or Bury than the Northern Quarter.

Manford's Comedy Club

Not a fixed venue but very much a fixture on the Manchester comedy calendar, Manford's Comedy Club is the touring night set up by Salford's own Jason Manford, who personally curates every line-up. Rather than running out of one room, it moves between a handful of proper theatre-style spaces around Greater Manchester and the surrounding towns, with each show bringing four circuit comedians hand-picked by Manford himself: big-name TV faces mixed in with some of the best full-time touring acts in the country.

Regular stops on the Manford's circuit around Manchester include:

  • Chorley Little Theatre: one of the club's longest-standing homes, with shows running most months
  • Hope Mill Theatre: Ancoats' well-loved theatre and performance space, occasionally hosting Manford's Comedy Club as part of its wider programme
  • Cheadle Civic Social Club: a regular semi-rural stop just south of the city
  • Altrincham Garrick Playhouse: a historic community theatre and one of the club's established venues
  • Alderley Edge: comedy nights in the Cheshire market town, rounding out the club's reach across Greater Manchester's outskirts

Because it's a touring format rather than a single club, the lineup and dates shift from venue to venue, so it's worth checking manfordscomedyclub.com for what's on near you and grabbing tickets early. Unallocated seating and a reasonably priced ticket (typically £13-£15) mean shows tend to sell out well in advance.
