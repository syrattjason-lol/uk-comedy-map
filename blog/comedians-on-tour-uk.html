Comedians on Tour in the UK

Big arena names, brilliant theatre acts and the small-room discoveries worth catching.

There has never been a better time to catch live comedy in the UK. From arena tours by household names to intimate work in progress shows at your local club, the circuit is stacked right now, and there's genuinely something for every taste and budget. We've split this guide into three tiers, from the biggest arena names down to the smaller circuit acts quietly building a following one theatre and club at a time, so you can find a night that suits your budget and your sense of humour.

Arena and stadium names

The biggest names on the circuit, filling the country's largest venues.

Peter Kay

Still one of the biggest draws in British comedy, Peter Kay's Better Late Than Never tour has been rolling on for years and continues to sell out arenas across the UK and Ireland. Expect classic observational material, huge audience singalongs and the kind of warmth that's made him one of the country's most loved comedians for two decades.

Jimmy Carr

Carr's Laughs Funny tour keeps his no frills approach front and centre: rapid fire one liners and dark, precisely constructed jokes delivered with total confidence. His shows tend to move fast, so if you like your comedy dense and unrelenting, this is the one to catch.

Bill Bailey

Bill Bailey continues to tour with his trademark blend of musical comedy, offbeat observations and genuine multi instrumental talent. His shows are as much a performance as a stand-up set, and he remains one of the few comedians who can make an audience laugh and be genuinely impressed in the same breath.

Ricky Gervais

Gervais returns to the road from autumn with a brand new stand-up show, bringing his characteristically blunt, provocative style to UK theatres and arenas.

Theatre-level touring comedians

A step down from the arenas but still very much household names, these comedians tend to play mid to large theatres up and down the country.

Jason Manford

A true household name, Jason Manford tours extensively across the UK with warm, relatable observational comedy that finds the funny side of everyday life. Alongside his solo tours, Manford also curates his own touring night, Manford's Comedy Club, which brings a rotating bill of circuit comedians to theatres in towns and cities that don't always get the big names through their doors.

Romesh Ranganathan

Known for his dry, deadpan wit, Romesh Ranganathan continues to be one of the most in demand acts on the circuit, with a new tour that leans into his knack for making the everyday feel absurd. Tickets for his shows tend to move fast, so it's worth booking early.

Chris Ramsey

Off the back of a sell out run with his last tour, Chris Ramsey is back on the road with a brand new show, bringing his warm, self deprecating style to theatres across the country.

Sophie Duker

Off the back of an Edinburgh Comedy Award Best Newcomer nomination and a Taskmaster win, Sophie Duker is currently touring her new show, taking her sharp, confident stand-up to venues across the UK and Ireland.

Maisie Adam

Known for her quick wit on panel shows, Maisie Adam is out on the road with a new hour that leans into her relatable, self deprecating style. Her tours tend to move through mid-size theatres, making for a more intimate night than the arena circuit.

Susan Calman

After a decade away from touring, Susan Calman is back on the road with a brand new show, giving fans a rare chance to catch her live stand-up rather than her TV work.

Club and circuit touring acts

This is where the UK's comedy scene really shows its depth. These comedians might not be filling arenas yet, but they're some of the sharpest, funniest and most inventive acts working today, and catching them in a small theatre or club is often a better night out than a big arena show at a fraction of the price.

Phil Ellis

A Taskmaster breakout star and multi award nominated comic, Phil Ellis brings a chaotic, distinctive brand of stand-up to clubs and small theatres across the country on his first full national tour. Expect something genuinely unpredictable rather than a standard club set.

Rob Auton

One of the most original voices on the circuit, Rob Auton built his reputation on a series of Edinburgh Fringe hours each dedicated to a single word or idea. His current tour marks his first full narrative story, and it's well worth seeking out if you like your comedy a little more thoughtful and offbeat than the norm.

Stephen Bailey

A regular face on Live at the Apollo and Would I Lie to You, Stephen Bailey tours smaller theatres and comedy clubs with his sharp, gossipy, crowd led style, and he's built a reputation as one of the hardest working comedians on the circuit.

Circuit regulars worth seeking out

Beyond the names with their own solo tours, a huge amount of the UK's best comedy happens on mixed bills at small theatres, social clubs and pub venues, where circuit regulars like Nick Doody, Alistair Barrie and Steve Gribbin headline rotating line ups alongside new material from the next generation of acts. These shows rarely make the big listings sites, so it's worth following local promoters and small venues directly to catch them.

Where to catch work in progress shows

If arena prices aren't your thing, keep an eye on your local comedy club rather than just the big venues. Many touring comedians use smaller rooms across the country for work in progress shows in the months before a tour is announced, which means you can often catch a big name for a fraction of the price while they're still finding the jokes. Edinburgh Festival Fringe in August is the biggest hotspot for this, but comedians regularly drop into circuit clubs year round to test new material ahead of a run.

How to keep track of who's touring near you

New tour dates are announced constantly, and popular shows sell out fast, so it pays to check regularly rather than waiting until a comedian is already in your city. Official box offices and venue websites are the safest bet for accurate pricing and avoiding resale markups, and it's always worth following your favourite comedians directly on social media, since that's usually where new dates and extra shows get announced first. For the circuit level acts in particular, following your local independent comedy club is often the quickest way to find out who's coming to town.
